## Argument development (theorem stating)

Argument development resolves each accepted goal by proof or disproof, building a useful supporting DAG and rigorous NL arguments for the selected direction. Additional mathematical definitions and observations may extend the model's vocabulary while preserving its accepted meanings.

The audit selects a change between proving a goal and proving its exact negation when the evidence warrants it. The reviewer directs broad DAG construction or focused repair. Workers develop arguments, create supporting nodes, and revise statements and dependencies within the authorized scope. A useful decomposition makes distinct subarguments independently checkable and gives later Lean formalization a workable structure. Revised statements receive fresh semantic review.

Proof-bearing nodes contain corresponding Lean and NL statements with rigorous NL arguments. Their logical support is represented by dependencies, and their steps are justified in enough detail for formalization. Lean proofs may remain provisional.

Completion requires current goal-faithfulness, node substantiveness and correspondence, and an approved NL argument or accepted Lean proof for every proof-bearing node. Definitions are complete, the DAG is well formed, and Lean compiles with provisional proofs permitted. With no unresolved findings, the reviewer advances to Lean proof formalization.
